How This Calculator Works

Every personal loan EMI in India, from every bank and NBFC, uses the same reducing-balance formula:

EMI = P × R × (1+R)^N / ((1+R)^N − 1)

Where P is your loan amount, R is the monthly interest rate (annual rate divided by 12), and N is the total number of monthly installments. This calculator applies that exact formula, so the number you see here should match what any lender's own calculator shows for the same inputs.

Why Personal Loan Rates Run So High

Personal loans are unsecured, no property, no vehicle, no asset backs the loan. That's exactly why the rate sits well above a home loan or loan against property: the lender is taking on more risk with nothing to reclaim if you default, so the rate compensates for that. If you have a genuine asset available, even a smaller one, it's always worth checking what a secured option would cost instead before settling on an unsecured personal loan.

Common Questions

How is personal loan EMI calculated?

Using the reducing-balance formula shown above, the same one every bank and NBFC in India uses.

Why are personal loan rates higher than home loan rates?

Personal loans are unsecured, so lenders charge a higher rate to offset the risk of having no collateral to claim if you default.

What EMI can I actually afford?

Most lenders want your total EMI obligations, including this loan, to stay under 50-55% of your net monthly income. Beyond that, both approval odds and your monthly budget take a real hit.
