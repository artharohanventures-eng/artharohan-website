Quick Summary
  • Need immediate possession? → Resale
  • Budget matters more than timeline? → New Construction (lower entry price, but GST applies)
  • Want to avoid legal paperwork risk? → New Construction from a reputed, RERA-registered builder

A fact most buyers don't know: Delhi's stamp duty is 6% for a male buyer, 4% for a female buyer, and 5% for joint male-female ownership — calculated on whichever is higher, the sale price or the government circle rate. Registering the property in a woman's name (solely or jointly) is a real, legal way to reduce this cost — worth considering before you finalise how the sale deed is structured.

What to actually verify before buying resale in Delhi

  • 1

    Full chain of title — not just the current sale deed. Delhi resale requires tracing ownership back through every previous transfer, not a single document.

  • 2

    Freehold conversion — many West Delhi properties are DDA-allotted. Confirm a registered Conveyance Deed exists proving leasehold-to-freehold conversion; without it, banks hesitate to finance the purchase.

  • 3

    Title search on both portals — Delhi has no formal Encumbrance Certificate. Check DORIS ↗ (records 2005–Jan 2024) and NGDRS Delhi ↗ (Jan 2024 onward) together, plus certified copies from the Sub-Registrar for anything older.

  • 4

    Mutation records at MCD — mutation should have followed every past transfer. A gap in the mutation trail is a real red flag, not a paperwork formality.

  • 5

    No loan or mortgage pending — confirmed via the title search, not just the seller's word.

  • 6

    Society NOC and dues — clear all pending maintenance before transfer, and confirm the transfer charge in writing before you agree on final price.

  • 7

    Never rely on a GPA-based sale — the Supreme Court (Suraj Lamp case) has ruled a General Power of Attorney does not convey ownership. Only a registered Sale Deed does.

  • 8

    Fair price — checked against the property's actual age and condition, not just the asking price.
