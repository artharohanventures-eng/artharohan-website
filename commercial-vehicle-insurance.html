The Basics

GCV vs PCV Insurance: What's the Difference?

Commercial vehicle insurance is broadly divided into Goods Carrying Vehicles (GCV) and Passenger Carrying Vehicles (PCV). Understanding the distinction matters because the vehicle category affects the applicable insurance category and the way third-party premium is determined.

🚛 What is GCV Insurance?

GCV stands for Goods Carrying Vehicle. GCV insurance applies to commercial vehicles used to transport goods. Examples include trucks, tempos, pickups, mini-trucks, lorries and other goods-carrying vehicles.


For goods-carrying vehicles, the applicable third-party category can depend on factors such as Gross Vehicle Weight (GVW) and whether the vehicle is being used as a public or private carrier.


Common GCV examples: trucks, pickups, tempos, mini-trucks and goods-carrying three-wheelers.

🚌 What is PCV Insurance?

PCV stands for Passenger Carrying Vehicle. PCV insurance applies to commercial vehicles used to transport passengers, including taxis, cabs, auto-rickshaws, e-rickshaws and buses.


For passenger-carrying vehicles, the applicable category can depend on the vehicle type and, for relevant categories, the licensed seating capacity.


Common PCV examples: taxis, cabs, auto-rickshaws, e-rickshaws, school/staff buses and other passenger-carrying vehicles.

GCV vs PCV in simple terms: GCV means the vehicle primarily carries goods; PCV means the vehicle primarily carries passengers. Do not choose the insurance category based only on the vehicle's appearance. The registered classification and permitted use matter.

Public Carrier vs Private Carrier

Public Carrier

A goods vehicle used for transporting goods for hire or reward falls into the public-carrier context. The applicable insurance and permit requirements depend on the vehicle's registered category and use.

Private Carrier

A private carrier is generally used to transport goods connected with the owner's own business rather than goods carried for hire or reward. The exact legal and insurance treatment should be confirmed from the vehicle's registration and applicable transport requirements.

Mandatory Cover

Commercial Vehicle Third-Party Insurance

Third-party liability insurance is mandatory for vehicles plying on public roads in India. It is designed to cover legal liability arising from injury or damage caused to third parties, subject to the terms, conditions and limits of the applicable policy.

What Third-Party Insurance Covers

  • Liability arising from injury to third parties
  • Liability for third-party property damage subject to applicable limits
  • Mandatory liability protection for vehicles used on public roads

What It Does Not Cover

  • Damage to your own vehicle under a liability-only policy
  • Theft of your own vehicle
  • Own-damage repairs following an accident
  • Other own-damage benefits unless separately covered

GCV and PCV both matter here. Commercial third-party insurance is not one single rate for every vehicle. The applicable category depends on the vehicle classification and relevant regulatory parameters. When Should You Consider Comprehensive Cover?

Third-party insurance satisfies the mandatory liability requirement, but it does not protect your own vehicle against its own-damage risks. For a vehicle that earns income every day, the financial impact of an accident, theft or other covered loss can extend beyond the repair bill because the vehicle may also be unavailable for work.

Whether comprehensive cover makes sense depends on the vehicle's value, age, usage, risk exposure, deductible, IDV, insurer terms and the cost of the additional cover. Compare the actual policy terms rather than choosing only on premium.

No Claim Bonus

Your NCB Ladder, And Why It's Worth Protecting

No Claim Bonus is a discount on your own-damage premium for every claim-free year, it doesn't apply to the mandatory third-party portion. It resets to zero the moment you make an own-damage claim. NCB applies to the own-damage portion and the applicable slab should be checked against the policy terms in force for your vehicle.

Two Separate Requirements

Insurance and Permit Are Not the Same Thing

This trips up a lot of commercial vehicle owners, having valid insurance does not mean you're legally clear to operate for hire.

A permit from the regional transport authority is required separately from insurance for any commercial vehicle used for hire, public carrier goods vehicles, taxis, autos, and route buses. Insurance covers financial liability; the permit is your legal authorization to operate commercially. Both must be valid at all times, and neither substitutes for the other.

If you're a Private Carrier (goods vehicle used only for your own business, not for hire), permit requirements differ, worth confirming your exact category with us before assuming either way.

What Actually Moves Your Premium

What Insurers Actually Check

Gross Vehicle Weight (GVW)

Third-party rates step up in weight slabs set by IRDAI, heavier vehicles cost more to insure. Whether you're a Public or Private Carrier also changes the applicable rate.

Seating Capacity (PCV)

Buses and vans are priced largely by seating capacity. Taxis and auto-rickshaws sit in their own distinct rate categories.

Usage Intensity

A vehicle in daily commercial use faces materially higher accident exposure than an occasional or backup vehicle, this matters most for whether comprehensive is worth it.

Claims History

A recent own-damage claim affects your No Claim Bonus on the comprehensive portion, same as private vehicles, this is worth checking before your next repair decision.

Said Plainly, Once

Why There's No Rate Table On This Page

Commercial vehicle third-party rates are genuinely more variable than private car or bike rates, and change more often.

Third-party rates for commercial vehicles are fixed by IRDAI based on your exact Gross Vehicle Weight or seating capacity, and are revised periodically, sometimes with meaningful gaps between what's commonly cited online and what's currently in force.
