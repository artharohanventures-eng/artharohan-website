How This Calculator Works

Compound interest grows your money on both the original amount and all previously earned interest, not just the original amount alone. This calculator uses the standard annual compounding formula:

A = P × (1+r)^t

Where P is your principal (starting amount), r is the annual interest rate as a decimal, and t is the number of years. This is the standard mathematical formula commonly used to illustrate compound growth, using annual compounding as the convention.

Why Time Matters More Than Rate

Try the years slider at 5, then 20, then 40, keeping the rate fixed. The growth isn't linear, it accelerates, because each year's interest starts earning interest of its own. This is why starting early, even with a smaller amount, often outperforms starting later with more money. Compound interest rewards time in the market far more than it rewards timing the market.

Common Questions

What is the compound interest formula?

A = P × (1+r)^t, using annual compounding — shown in full above.

What's the difference between compound and simple interest?

Simple interest is calculated only on the original principal each year. Compound interest is calculated on the principal plus all previously earned interest, so growth accelerates over time.

Is this calculator for a specific investment product?

No. It's a generic, illustrative tool showing how compound growth works mathematically. It doesn't represent any specific deposit, bond, or investment product's guaranteed return.
