Pick the product by what the money is for

The same trader can need a term loan this year and a cash credit limit next year. The right product follows the need, not the other way round. In our files, growing the shop or the business is what traders ask for most often.

Growing the shop or business

A new outlet, a wider range of stock, equipment. This is a one-time need, so a term loan with a fixed EMI usually fits.

A gap that keeps coming back

You pay suppliers first and customers pay later, every month. A cash credit or overdraft fits better, because you draw what you need and repay when payments arrive.

One big stock purchase

For a single large buy, a short term loan can work. Match the repayment to when the stock turns into cash.

Our lenders offer both a term loan and a cash credit or overdraft, and we pick according to your need.

Term loan or cash credit: how they differ

Term loan

You get the full amount once and repay it in fixed EMIs. Interest runs on the whole amount from day one, whether or not you have used all of it. Our lenders offer it without collateral to GST-registered businesses with a clean credit profile.

Cash credit or overdraft

You get a limit and draw from it as needed. Interest runs only on the amount you use, for the days you use it. Lenders more often ask for security, though unsecured limits exist for strong profiles. Some lenders charge fees on the sanctioned limit.

Why taking more than you need costs you

The most useful advice we give a trader before applying: do not take more than you need. On a term loan, every extra rupee is paid for from the first day.

How much, and for how long

Every trading business is different, so we do not quote one amount or one tenure. These rules of thumb help you reach your own.

  1. Write the need in rupees. Use the actual purchase list or the equipment quote. Do not round up.
  2. Work out your cash cycle. Count the days between paying your supplier and getting paid by your customer. A long gap points to a cash credit or overdraft.
  3. Choose the tenure from your cash flow. Pick an EMI your business can pay in a slow month, not only in a good one.
  4. Compare the total you repay. Look at the EMI, the interest over the whole tenure and the fee, not just the headline rate.
  5. Apply once, to a lender that fits. Several applications add several credit enquiries. Send us your slip and we choose.

Working capital loan questions, answered honestly

What is a working capital loan for a trader?

It is money to run and grow the trading business, such as buying more stock, covering the gap between paying suppliers and getting paid, or expanding the shop. It can come as a term loan with a fixed EMI or as a cash credit or overdraft limit, depending on the need.

Term loan or cash credit: which should I take?

It depends on what the money is for. A one-time expansion usually fits a term loan with a fixed EMI. A gap that keeps coming back between buying and getting paid usually fits a cash credit or overdraft, where you pay interest only on what you use. We look at your profile and tell you which fits.

Can I get a working capital loan without collateral?

Yes, unsecured business loans are available for GST-registered businesses with a good track record and a clean credit profile. Cash credit and overdraft limits more often ask for security, though unsecured limits exist for strong profiles. The lender decides.

How much working capital loan should I take?

Only as much as you need. The right amount is different for every business, so add up the actual need in rupees, for example the purchase list or the equipment quote, and do not round up. A bigger loan means a bigger EMI and more interest from the first day on a term loan.

For how long should I take it?

There is no single right period. It depends on what the money is for and on your monthly cash flow. Choose a tenure whose EMI your business can pay comfortably in a slow month, and check it with the EMI calculator before you apply.
