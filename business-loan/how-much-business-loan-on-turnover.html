There is no fixed ratio between turnover and loan

Many people search for a formula, such as a loan equal to so many months of sales. In our files there is no single formula. The amount a lender offers depends on the lender and on your file. What does stay the same is what lenders look at to work out a number.

How big the sales are

In our files, the lenders we work with typically look for around Rs 10 lakh in monthly sales for an unsecured business loan. Some ask for more and some for less.

How long and how regular

GST registration and the last 12 months of GST returns, filed regularly, so the lender can see a steady track record.

Whether your papers agree

Lenders compare your GST returns, your bank statements and your ITR against each other. The three should tell the same story.

For what lenders read in the returns and the full document list, see the GST-based business loan guide.

The three papers that prove your turnover

GST returns

The sales you have declared to the government, month by month. Regular filing for the last 12 months is what lenders ask for.

Bank statements

The money that actually came into your business account. Lenders read it next to your GST sales.

ITR

Your income tax return for the business. Lenders hold it up against the other two.

None of the three is enough alone. A lender looks at all three together, so check that they agree before you apply.

Start from the EMI you can afford, not the amount you want

The most useful step before you decide how much to ask for: work out the monthly payment your business can carry, including in a slow month. Then work backwards to the loan.

If your turnover is on the low side

Ask for a smaller amount, one your sales can comfortably support. This is the first thing we tell a business with lower sales. A modest request that fits your numbers is easier for a lender to approve than a large one that does not.

The most common turnover problem we see is the opposite: asking for too big an amount. The loan asked for is out of proportion to the sales, and the file weakens even when everything else is in order. If a lender has already said no, read what to do after a rejection.

Business loan and turnover questions, answered honestly

How much business loan can I get on my turnover?

There is no fixed ratio. The amount depends on the lender and on your file: your GST returns, bank statements, ITR and credit history. We do not quote one figure for every business. Send us your details and we tell you a realistic range for your file.

What turnover do lenders look for in a business loan?

It differs by lender. In our files, the lenders we work with typically look for around Rs 10 lakh in monthly sales for an unsecured business loan, along with GST registration and the last 12 months of GST returns. Some lenders ask for more and some for less.

Which papers prove my turnover?

Lenders compare three sets of papers against each other: your GST returns, your bank statements and your ITR. The three should tell the same story. If they do not agree, the file gets weaker, so check them against each other before you apply.

What should I do if my turnover is low?

Ask for a smaller amount, one your sales can comfortably support. A modest request that fits your numbers is easier for a lender to say yes to than a large one that does not. We can look at your file and tell you what is realistic.

What is the most common mistake with turnover and loan amount?

Asking for too big an amount. The loan asked for is out of proportion to the business's sales, and the file weakens even when everything else is in order.

How do I decide how much to ask for?

Start from the EMI your business can pay every month, including in a slow month, and work backwards to the loan amount. Use the business loan EMI calculator to check the figures before you apply.
