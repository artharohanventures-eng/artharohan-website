How a lender reads your GST returns

For a GST-based loan, your returns do the job that collateral does in a secured loan. The lender is not checking one number. It is checking whether the story across 12 months holds together.

Your declared sales, month by month

The GSTR-3B is your monthly summary return. Lenders read the trend across the year, so a steady line counts for more than one big month.

Whether you filed every month, on time

A missed or nil return makes a real business look weak. Recent months matter most.

Whether the bank agrees with the GST

Deposits in your business account are laid next to the sales you declared. A big gap invites questions.

How long you have been filing

The lenders we work with look at the last 12 months of returns. Some banks want more.

What our lenders ask for, in practice

These come from the files we handle, not from a brochure. Each lender sets its own rules, so treat them as a guide.

Monthly sales of around Rs 10 lakh or more

For an unsecured business loan, this is what most of our lenders look for. Below it you have fewer options, and we say so honestly before you spend time on documents.

12 months of GST returns

All filed, with the recent months on time.

3 to 7 working days

The two most common reasons for a no

A low credit score with overdue accounts, and too many existing EMIs. Both can be seen before you apply.

Documents for a GST-based loan

  • GST registration certificate
  • GSTR-3B returns for the last 12 months, filed
  • Last 12 months of business bank statements
  • PAN and Aadhaar of the owner
  • Business address proof
  • Udyam certificate, if you have one
  • ITR, if you have filed it

Each lender has its own list. We tell you exactly which ones yours needs.

Keep your GST login to yourself

Never give your GST portal password to anyone. Download your returns as PDFs and send those. A lender or agent who asks for your login is a red flag.

No ITR? Some lenders will still look at GST returns and bank statements. See the full answer on the business loan page.

Five things to do before you apply

  1. File every pending return. Check that all 12 months show as filed and the recent ones were on time.
  2. Check your credit report and clear overdue accounts. A low score is the most common reason for a no.
  3. Add up your existing EMIs. If they are heavy, close small loans and card dues first.
  4. Compare your bank deposits with your declared sales. Route business sales through the business account so the two tell the same story.
  5. Apply to one lender that fits, not to many. Each full application usually adds a credit enquiry, and several in a short time can lower your score. Send us your slip and we choose the lender, so you apply once.

GST-based loan questions, answered honestly

Can I get a business loan on GST returns alone?

Some lenders assess a business loan on your GST returns and business bank statements, without asking for ITR. Your options are fewer and the rate is often higher. Approval is never guaranteed, and the lender makes the final decision.

How many months of GST returns do lenders need?

The lenders we work with look at your last 12 months of GST returns. Some banks ask for more. Every month should be filed, and the recent ones should be on time.

What monthly sales do I need for an unsecured business loan?

In our experience, lenders offering unsecured business loans usually look for monthly GST sales of around Rs 10 lakh or more. Below that you will have fewer options. This is a rule of thumb, not a guarantee, and each lender sets its own limit.

How long does approval take?

Usually 3 to 7 working days from the day a complete file is submitted. A missing return or an unclear bank statement is what stretches it. The lender decides the timeline.

Will applying to many lenders at once hurt my chances?

It can. A full loan application usually leads to a credit enquiry on your report, and several in a short time can lower your score and make lenders cautious. It is better to apply to one lender that fits your profile than to send the same file to many.
