How the SIP Calculator Works

A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) lets you invest a fixed amount every month into a mutual fund, instead of investing a lump sum all at once. This calculator uses the standard compound growth formula for a series of regular monthly investments:

FV = P × [((1+i)^n − 1) / i] × (1+i)

Where P is your monthly investment, i is the monthly rate of return (annual rate divided by 12), and n is the total number of months invested. This is the exact same formula used industry-wide, including by AMFI-registered distributors and every major fund house.

Why the Return Assumption Matters

The single biggest lever in this calculator isn't the monthly amount, it's the return percentage you assume. A 2% difference in assumed annual return can change your 20-year projection by lakhs of rupees. There is no guaranteed return on any mutual fund SIP; 12% is a commonly cited illustrative figure based on long-term historical Indian equity averages, but it is not a promise. Try the slider at 8%, 10%, and 15% to see how sensitive your outcome really is to this one number, before you plan around any single figure.

Common Questions

How is SIP return calculated?

Using the compound interest formula for a series of regular monthly investments (shown above), applied consistently across the industry.

Is 12% a realistic SIP return assumption?

It's a commonly used illustrative figure, not a guarantee. Actual returns depend on the specific fund, market conditions, and the exact period invested, and can be meaningfully higher or lower.

Does Artharohan recommend specific mutual funds?

No. This is a generic, illustrative calculator only. It doesn't recommend any specific fund or scheme. For fund selection or personalized investment advice, consult a SEBI-registered investment adviser.
